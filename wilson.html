Cure · Wilson disease · Computational hepatology

A cure for Wilson disease: how much of the liver must be corrected?

The widely quoted 20–30% corrected-liver-cell target falls short for the compound heterozygotes a first-in-human prime-editing trial enrolls: it delivers a capacity of 0.10–0.15, below the fifth percentile of the 0.22 threshold derived here. The cure needs closer to 43% of alleles corrected, and this paper shows where that number comes from.

Cure A Disease · November 2026

Scope. Cure A Disease presents gene editing of ATP7B as a cure for Wilson disease. This paper is a computational study. No experiment was performed; nothing reported here has been tested in cells, animals or humans. It estimates the design target a cure must hit and states which human data would test it. The editing therapy discussed (PM577) belongs to its sponsor, Prime Medicine, not to Cure A Disease.

Abstract

A prime-editing cure for Wilson disease (ATP7B) has entered a first-in-human trial enrolling patients with at least one p.H1069Q allele. The sponsor’s guiding statement is that correcting 20–30% of hepatocytes may be curative; we found no peer-reviewed derivation. We derive the requirement from published physiology. A fixed-intake copper mass balance gives a threshold of 0.71 of normal ATP7B capacity, which contradicts the cure of Wilson disease by transplantation of carrier-parent livers (58 recipients, no recurrence), so it is rejected. A steady-state hepatic copper store model, calibrated to carrier liver copper (153 µg/g) and bounded by injury-range copper, gives a threshold of 0.33 (5–95%: 0.22–0.43). Because 74–82% of patients are compound heterozygotes, a corrected hepatocyte has carrier-level function, so capacity equals half the fraction of H1069Q alleles corrected when the partner allele is null. Correcting 20–30% of cells then yields a capacity of only 0.10–0.15. A Monte Carlo conditioned on untreated patients being ill puts the required per-allele correction at a median 0.43 (5–95%: 0.10–0.73). The probability that 51% correction suffices is 0.65 under the derived threshold and 1.0 under a mouse-anchored threshold of 0.20. The largest uncertainty is partner-allele activity. The model is calibrated on one carrier measurement and assumes a well-mixed liver; it is a prediction to be tested against trial data.

1 Introduction

Wilson disease is a recessive failure of hepatic copper export. Prime Medicine’s PM577 (NCT07748403; first patient dosed October 2026) corrects the H1069Q allele and enrolls patients with at least one copy. The therapeutic threshold cited for it, correction of 20–30% of hepatocytes, appears only in a conference poster.1 Two features of the enrolled population make that number hard to interpret. Most patients are compound heterozygotes,2 so editing one allele cannot restore wild-type function. And reported editing efficiencies are per allele, measured in bulk tissue, mostly in non-disease contexts.1 We ask what whole-liver capacity is sufficient for a cure, and what correction a compound heterozygote needs to reach it.

Where this happens

2 Model

Mass balance fails, and for an instructive reason. Healthy copper balance closes (residual −368 µg/day against a gross biliary SD of ±800) only with true absorption (66%);3 apparent absorption (36%)4 already nets out endogenous secretion and fails by 784 µg/day. Even then, requiring net retention of zero or less at fixed intake gives a threshold of 0.711. Carrier-parent livers, running near 0.50 capacity, cure recipients (58 reported, no recurrence),5 so 0.711 is rejected: a fixed-intake balance ignores that copper stores adjust.

Copper-store model. At steady state, hepatic copper is C = C0·cap−1/n, with excretion sublinear in the store (n < 1). The exponent n is calibrated so a carrier liver (capacity 0.37–0.50) sits at the measured 153 µg/g dry weight,6 against a normal 20–50. The curative threshold is the capacity at which the store reaches injury-range copper (200–300 µg/g; the diagnostic value is 2507): capcrit = (C0/Ctox)n. Wilson patients, with capacity near 0, have no steady state below injury.

Editing to capacity. For H1069Q/other, whole-liver capacity at per-allele correction a is 0.5[a + (1−a)ph] + 0.5po, with partner activity po (triangular 0–0.5, mode 0) and residual H1069Q activity ph (0–0.25).8 With a null partner, capacity is exactly a/2: a factor-of-two penalty relative to biallelic correction at every threshold. Draws in which untreated patients would already be above threshold are discarded (382,181 of 400,000 retained).

3 Validation

Ten checks were specified; all ten pass, but they are not equally strong. Independent: mass balance closes on true and fails on apparent absorption (V1–V2); the fixed-intake threshold contradicts carrier cure and is rejected (V3); the store-model threshold respects the carrier bound in 99.9% of draws (V4); the sublinear exponent is physical (median 0.56, V10); sufficiency is monotone in editing (V9). Not independent: V5 reproduces the carrier copper that calibrated it, and V6 and V7 hold by construction of priors and constraint. Check V8, that 20–30% with a null partner lies below the model’s fifth percentile (0.219), is a result and not a test.

4 Results

The cure threshold is 0.33 of normal capacity (5–95%: 0.22–0.43), inside the span of published targets (0.15–0.40; one mouse study 0.20) and below the 0.50 carrier bound.

The 20–30% figure is correct only for biallelic correction. Biallelic correction at 20–30% gives capacity 0.20–0.30. For a compound heterozygote with a null partner it gives 0.10–0.15, at or below the lowest published target.

Required correction is about 43% of H1069Q alleles (5–95%: 10–73%). Sufficiency probability is 0.14, 0.44, 0.65, 0.92 and 0.98 at 20, 40, 51, 70 and 80% correction (Table 1), and rises from 0.64 to 1.0 across 20–40% under the 0.20 anchor. The 51% already reported by the sponsor in primates sits comfortably inside the cure zone under the mouse-anchored threshold.

Partner-allele activity dominates. Rank correlation with required editing is −0.59, followed by carrier liver copper (+0.47) and carrier capacity (+0.39). Patients whose second allele retains function need far less; null-null-like genotypes need more.

Selective repopulation cannot be quantified from existing data. The only fit (rat, preconditioned) gives 7.75 per year, an uninformative upper bound. Growing the corrected fraction from 0.3 to 0.6 in five years would need s = 0.25 per year.

5 Limitations

One calibration datum. The threshold rests on two published carrier liver copper means6 and an assumed power-law excretion form; a different functional form would move it. Well-mixed liver. Capacity is averaged across cells. Copper toxicity is partly cell-autonomous, so uncorrected cells may be injured regardless, which would raise the requirement; the 20% mouse result9 points the other way. We do not resolve this. Retracted earlier estimate. A preliminary 1.5-fold alleles-versus-cells penalty and a combined 2.98-fold gap are withdrawn: in a well-mixed liver capacity is linear in allele correction and the distinction cancels. The factor of two is exact. Inputs are thin. Biliary copper output rests on one 1977 study; primate prime-editing figures are sponsor-reported with undisclosed dose and n; no human liver editing measurement exists for any therapy; H1069Q residual activity is disputed.8 Scope. The analysis addresses hepatic copper only, not neurological disease, and covers H1069Q carriers only.

6 Testable predictions

  1. Compound heterozygotes with null partners and under 20% allele correction should not normalise urinary copper or exchangeable copper.
  2. Response should be graded in dose, with probability rising across 20–70% correction, and should stratify by partner-allele genotype.
  3. Successful correction should leave a carrier-like signature: hepatic copper near 150 µg/g (not near 35), elevated urinary copper, normal ceruloplasmin.
  4. Failure at 50% correction in null-partner patients would falsify the well-mixed model. The earlier gene-addition trial, terminated for futility with no responders among four,10 is a reminder that the threshold is demanding.
